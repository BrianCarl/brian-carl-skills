# Calibration examples

Use these to calibrate reasoning, not as fixed templates or established user beliefs.

## Takeaway missing before the method

Input: “I think of AI as doing, advising, or tracking. I'm realizing this explains how I think, but not what someone should do next.”

Next move: Propose the learner decision rather than immediately expanding the taxonomy. Ask whether the intended problem is delegating judgment, assigning work before defining the desired result, or overlooking uses beyond execution. These are different lessons; do not merge them into a larger checklist. After the intended problem is clear, develop criteria and a worked example.

Failure: Assume the takeaway is settled and ask only for more detail about the three labels.

## Review a long-form explanation with practical advice

Input: An article explains how a marketing channel is changing, argues that its foundations still matter, and ends with advice to format pages around customer questions. It already demonstrates question headings and direct answers in its own structure. The user asks for an educational review.

Next move: Recognize the existing explanation, recommendation, and embedded demonstration. Preserve the article's broader argument. Identify the application gap: which page to choose, how to revise it, and how to inspect the result. Propose a worked example as an addition rather than replace the article with a tutorial. Ask about prioritization only if it would change the author's recommendation.

For an illustrative before-and-after, compare the same customer question before and after making its answer explicit. Explain the changes and show a bounded exercise the reader can try. Distinguish improved clarity from an unmeasured claim about channel performance. Keep evidence corrections focused on claims underlying the recommended action.

Failure: Call the whole article opinion-only, silently narrow its promise, rewrite it without being asked, or turn the review into a broad channel audit.

## Practical kernel with missing criteria

Input: “The more critical the task, the less action you need from AI. Projects are AI does, AI advises, or AI tracks. AI can publish a webpage but should advise on strategy.”

Diagnosis: Three useful roles exist. “Critical” does not explain selection criteria; important work can contain execution. A project may combine roles.

Proposed outcome: Assign a role to a specific part of a project.

Useful discovery response: “The teaching kernel is choosing AI's role before assigning work. What's missing is how you choose. A candidate rule is: use AI to do work when you can specify the output and verify it; advise when you need to weigh tradeoffs; track when you need visibility into progress. Is that how you decide, or do you use different criteria?”

Ask what tracking monitors and reports only if needed for the lesson. Once criteria are confirmed, use a hypothetical website launch to demonstrate combined roles and explain each assignment through the rule. Do not claim all publishing is suitable for autonomous execution.

Failure: Improve the hook and repeat the three roles without teaching how to choose.

## Instruction present but vague

Input: “Generic AI answers aren't just a prompt problem. Add a context layer with memory, instructions, and skills.”

Diagnosis: An action exists, but what information to include, where it belongs, and how to check its effect remain unclear.

Next move: Propose an outcome about supplying relevant context for one recurring task. Ask which task to demonstrate and which missing context actually changes its output. Verify current product behavior before teaching platform-specific steps.

Failure: Invent the user's setup, list generic tools, or assume every generic answer has the same cause.

## Enough substance to draft

Input: “For a solo marketer deciding which webinar clips to publish, I score each clip on whether it answers a customer question, makes sense without the full webinar, and gives one usable takeaway. A clip failing any of those gets rewritten or dropped. Turn this into a short teaching post.”

Next move: Draft directly. Audience, decision, criteria, and resulting action are supplied. Use a clearly hypothetical clip if needed to demonstrate the reasoning. Preserve the rule rather than substitute an invented engagement model.

## Unsupported premise

Input: “Small marketing teams should automate everything because human review wastes time.”

Next move: Flag the universal claim and ask which work is meant. Propose a narrower hypothesis about repetitive, verifiable work. Do not create a confident tutorial for eliminating all review.
