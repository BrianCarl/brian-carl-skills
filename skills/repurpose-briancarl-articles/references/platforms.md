# Platform delivery

## X

- Default to a deliberately structured thread, usually 5–8 posts, but follow the material. Fewer strong posts beat padding; honor an explicit requested length.
- Let the first line carry the stake. Use the rest of post 1 for a credible payoff where helpful; avoid opening with "A thread."
- Put a useful payoff in post 2. Keep one main idea per post. Preserve enough context that a reader arriving midway can understand it.
- Number posts once. Include numbering in the length calculation. Treat `---` as a display separator, not part of published text.
- Stay within the standard 280 weighted-character budget. For basic text, count characters and reserve numbering; URLs are normally counted as 23 characters. Emoji and non-Latin characters require weighted treatment, so use an available trusted counter or leave sufficient margin and label estimates honestly. Do not equate Python `len()` with a formal platform check for all Unicode.
- End with a natural article/newsletter CTA and the actual URL. A later link keeps the first post focused; do not claim a universal algorithm penalty for links.
- Never add hashtags, emojis, hype, confessions, odd-precision numbers, or artificial fragments solely to look native or human.

## LinkedIn

- Default to one complete post, roughly 150–300 words when the article supports it. Prefer shorter when the point is simple; honor the user's requested length.
- Adapt the chosen angle to a professional reader. Keep the first line strong; use the next paragraph to establish context and consequence without repeating the hook.
- Retain an author-specific example or explanation and practical takeaway. Use natural paragraphs, and bullets only where they improve reading.
- Do not expand the X thread into an essay or strip its numbering and call that adaptation. Let LinkedIn carry nuance, experience, and explanation naturally.
- Put the article URL with the closing invitation by default, so conversion has a clear path. Offer a first-comment version only when asked or supported by the user's measured preferences; do not assert it guarantees better reach.
- Preserve Brian's point of view and words; avoid generic leadership lessons, ceremonial conclusions, and invented vulnerability.

## Measurement

Treat article visits and newsletter subscriptions as the business outcome. Use impressions and engagement to diagnose the funnel rather than replacing its goal. If tracking exists, retain it; add consistent source/medium/campaign parameters only when requested or already established. Never claim attribution or measured improvement without data.
